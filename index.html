My Background

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at La Rochelle Université, working within the L3i Laboratory in France. My research lies at the intersection of computer vision, document image analysis, optical character recognition, and scene text understanding.

Before starting my Ph.D., I completed my master's degree with research on word spotting in Khmer palm leaf manuscript documents at the Institute of Technology of Cambodia, where I also earned my engineering degree in Computer Science.

Current Research

My Ph.D. research focuses on end-to-end scene text spotting for Khmer script as a representative low-resource and complex writing system. I investigate the challenges involved in detecting and recognizing Khmer text in natural scenes and explore how modern deep learning approaches can be adapted to scripts for which large-scale annotated resources are limited.

My work includes the development of Khmer scene-text datasets, detection and recognition benchmarks, end-to-end text spotting approaches, and cross-lingual experiments designed to study generalization across low-resource languages.

An end-to-end scene text spotting approach designed to improve recognition of Khmer and other low-resource complex scripts.
